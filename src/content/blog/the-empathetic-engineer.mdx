For whatever reason, the best software engineers that I've ever worked with always have one thing in common: they are empathetic people.

The stereotypical software engineer isn't empathetic. Most people picture software developers as these bloated, greasy creatures living in basements and staring at computer monitors. We can barely hold a conversation, let alone put ourselves in someone else's shoes. 

While there are some cave-dwellers out there, in my experience, the best software engineers typically have a high degree of emotional intelligence, specifically empathy. Why is that?

## People Mindedness

One reason, I'd wager, is that empathy lets you see the world through another person's eyes. Being able to do this well is extremely helpful when building software. It also helps you spot problem areas faster. Highly empathetic engineers bring up latent UX issues long before users ever report them, becauase they're thinking about how a user interacts with their software at a personal level.

This sounds obvious but you'd be surprised at the number of developers who don't think this way. They prioritize adding "keyboard shortcuts" to a web application, for instance, even if their intended audience isn't that sophisticated and exclusively works with the mouse. They work in Vim, after all, so if the keyboard shortcuts are helpful _to them_ they must be helpful to others, right?

Being able to perform the digital equivalent of the "smell test" on software before or while it's being built is very valuable.

This type of empathy is often referred to as "product-mindedness," but I think that's a misnomer. It could be more accurately described as "people-mindedness." It's the ability to empathize with your users and anticipate their needs and complaints. 

This isn't to say that companies can survive on empathy and people-mindedness alone. I've worked places where leadership says they "hire for engineers who are product-minded," but invest nothing in product research, A/B testing, surveys, or experimentation. They think that engineers can understand their userbase so well that they don't have to spend time doing this legwork. This is naive. Just "being empathetic" will not substitute for analytics and customer research. But it's the special sauce that in my experience separates good engineers from really good ones. 

## Collaboration

Empathetic people are just easier to work with, as well.

Most obviously, it means they're better at making small talk during meetings, getting to know coworkers on a personal level, and navigating the oftentimes complex and divergent interests of the engineering team with the design team, sales team, and other company leaders. But empathetic engineers are also better at building up institutional influence for their ideas, and are therefore more effective overall.

I've seen plenty of highly technical, capable engineers flounder because they've built out a solution with zero regard for maintenance or documentation. Even if their solution is technically brilliant, they haven't taken the time to get buy-in from other people on the team, or consider how others will understand or maintain their code. They take no time to document or educate others on the approach, which ends up losing steam. As the author, you'll always have more insight into your code than the next person.
